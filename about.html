01 OUR STORY

MORE THAN A SCHOOL

A tradition built across generations.

Dale is a place where boys are challenged to become more capable, more courageous and more purposeful young men.

Dale College Boys' High School has been part of the Eastern Cape's story for generations. From its early beginnings as the Public Undenominational School for Boys, the College has developed a proud tradition founded on education, character and participation.

Today, that tradition continues. The classrooms, playing fields, halls and courtyards of Dale are places where friendships are formed, standards are established and memories are made.

02 OUR HISTORY

ROOTED IN THE EASTERN CAPE

Built on heritage.

1861
THE BEGINNING

A school is established.

Dale College begins life as the Public Undenominational School for Boys, establishing the foundations of a tradition that would endure for generations.

1877
FOUNDATION

A legacy set in stone.

Sir Bartle Frere, Governor of the Cape, lays the foundation stone — marking an important chapter in the College's development.

1908
A NEW HOME

The Herbert Baker building.

The school moves into its magnificent new home on Albert Road, designed by renowned architect Sir Herbert Baker.

1960
A NEW CHAPTER

The High School moves.

The High School relocates to Queen's Road, while the Junior School remains within the historic Herbert Baker building.

More than a century and a half later, the places may have changed, but the spirit remains.

1861 — PRESENT

04 OUR CAMPUS

A PLACE TO BELONG

High ground. Wide horizons.

Dale's campus is more than a collection of buildings. It is a shared environment where generations of boys have learned, competed, celebrated and grown together.

From classrooms and laboratories to sporting facilities, boarding houses and spaces for gathering, every part of campus contributes to the experience of being a Dale boy.
